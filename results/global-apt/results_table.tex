% Commentary on results
Looking at the results, GraphSAGE clearly stands out with a ROC-AUC of 0.97 and an F1-score of 0.86. What's interesting here is that it manages to catch almost all anomalies (99.3\% recall) while still maintaining decent precision (76.2\%), which is pretty rare on imbalanced datasets like this one.

The other algorithms (SLADE, GAT, NetWalk) all show a similar pattern - they catch almost everything (99-100\% recall) but generate tons of false positives. I guess that's a design choice - better to have too many alerts than miss a real anomaly, especially in network security. Though it can be annoying in production if you have to manually check everything.

GeneralDYG is clearly struggling here. It predicts almost everything as normal, which makes it pretty much useless out of the box. Probably needs some hyperparameter tuning or a different loss function, but we didn't dig deeper into that.

One thing we discovered that's worth mentioning: using a default threshold of 0.5 doesn't work at all. We had to automatically optimize thresholds for each algorithm, and that completely changed the results. GraphSAGE works better with a threshold around 0.61, while GAT needs a really low threshold (0.01) to catch all anomalies. This threshold optimization was crucial - without it, most algorithms would just predict everything as normal.
